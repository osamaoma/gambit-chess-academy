# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Club and improving chess players (roughly 800–2000 rating) who want a structured way to learn openings instead of memorizing engine dumps. They arrive between games — on a laptop at home or a phone on a commute — with 10–30 minutes to study. The job to be done: "teach me the ideas of this opening, drill me until the moves are automatic, and tell me when to review."

## Product Purpose

Gambit Chess Academy is a premium chess learning platform: curated opening courses with per-move coaching notes, spaced-repetition review (SM-2-lite), a course creator, and a chess.com-grade game review tool. Success = a learner can walk any published line to the end, explain the plan behind it, and come back when reviews are due. Built-in courses focus on the highest-probability variations (30–50 lines per course), not thousands of micro-sub-variations — depth of explanation over breadth of coverage.

## Brand Personality

Masterful, warm, exacting. The voice of a strong coach who loves the game: confident, plain-spoken, never condescending. Visually: dark monochrome + gold ("Chessmasters" theme), Clash Display / Satoshi / DM Mono, brown board with Cburnett pieces. The interface should feel like a private study in a grand chess club — quiet, focused, premium.

## Anti-references

- Chessable's wall-of-text line lists and quantity-first course marketing ("100,000 trainable variations").
- Generic SaaS dashboards: cream backgrounds, gradient text, identical card grids, hero-metric templates.
- Toy chess sites: pixel pieces, primary-color boards, gamification confetti.
- Anything that reads as templated or AI-generated rather than designed.

## Design Principles

1. **The move explains itself** — every ply a learner sees carries one clear coaching sentence; no naked move lists.
2. **Depth over breadth** — fewer, better lines; a course is a repertoire you can actually finish.
3. **The board is the hero** — layout, color, and motion defer to the position on the board.
4. **Quiet luxury** — gold is an accent, not a costume; restraint is the brand.
5. **Respect the returning learner** — due reviews, progress, and continue-CTAs surface before new content.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA contrast targets on text (dark theme: verify gold-on-dark and gray body text ≥ 4.5:1).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected on all animations (already a codebase convention).
- Keyboard operability for board controls is a stated roadmap item; new UI should not regress focus visibility.
